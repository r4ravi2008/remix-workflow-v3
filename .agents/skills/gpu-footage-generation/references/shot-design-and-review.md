# Footage that an editor can use

Design each shot for its placement, not merely a pleasing first frame. Record narrative state, camera action, subject action, required duration and clean handles. Use one controllable action per take when complexity threatens identity. Plan compatible scales, eyelines, screen directions and lighting across neighboring shots.

For fast fashion/music-video cutting, create complementary close, medium, wide, reaction, detail and environmental coverage as the brief needs. Give the editor several stable moments and clean entry/exit motion; avoid embedding a full multi-cut montage or all lighting accents inside every generated take. Do beat-exact flashes, typography and repeatable geometric transitions in the editor unless generation itself is the requested effect. Review flashing intensity and readability in playback.

Preserve the accepted performance and story role. A deadpan abstract club insert should not become choreographed dancing or relocate a narrative scene into the club. A slow approach must not start with the characters already intimate. Faces looking at each other while walking can produce unnatural blocking; choose a readable action and camera position. A no-singing brief needs relaxed closed-mouth performance and inspection of the entire usable range.

Keep visual prompts free of pipeline instructions such as “leave space for DaVinci message graphics” or “add lyrics later.” Urvashi's C014 generated white lettering from editing-related prompt language. Put such instructions in `editor_notes`, excluded from the model payload. Describe visible geometry instead: composition, negative space, inward-facing phone, relaxed lips, wardrobe, light and a simple motion. Keep generated UI text out unless explicitly required and reviewable.

For first/last-frame conditioning, verify both anchors and actual endpoint match. Different locations or story states may need an editorial cut rather than a forced morph. A seamless request still requires visual seam review, including velocity and action; a small pixel difference cannot certify natural movement. A long tail needs real continuing footage, not a repeated endpoint. Preserve all raw frames before any trim/conform.

## Accept by intended use

Probe and fully decode the original, inspect head/middle/tail and focal details, then watch the whole proposed usable range at normal speed. Compare against the anchors and neighbors. Check faces/hands, extra people, mouth motion, unintended text, environment drift, subject visibility, camera behavior, lighting flicker, static holds, endpoint continuity and length after handles.

Black/freeze detectors are diagnostic. Distinct pixels can still be a static picture with compression noise; low motion can be a deliberate restrained performance. Adding a push to a frozen take is an editorial animation, not evidence of generated subject motion. Classify honestly under the current brief. Trimming to a clean prefix creates a new accepted range; bad later frames cannot serve as transition handles.

An upscale is a separately reviewed derivative, not a repair for failed identity or action. Preserve the native source and time map; compare at identical delivery dimensions and check faces, texture flicker, edge halos and any chunk overlap in motion. Measure the pilot through decoding and final save on the actual GPU before batching. Follow the director's [upscaling reference](../../music-video-director/references/upscaling-and-restoration.md) for selective application and Resolve handoff.

Retiming must preserve usable action. Never duplicate frames, interpolate a warped face, or stretch a short clip through a verse solely to fill coverage. Reuse a source range deliberately with an evolved role/order/composition, and record repetition separately. Rejected takes stay excluded from accepted duration even when their downloads and technical scans pass.

Download the pilot promptly for review before further paid work. Use the director's configurable quality policy when available; otherwise record concrete pass/fail observations and the exact accepted range. Under autonomous mode, the agent inspects and continues. Under a user-set pilot gate, provide the actual local file and stop compute while waiting. Missing playback capability leaves a review candidate, not accepted footage.
